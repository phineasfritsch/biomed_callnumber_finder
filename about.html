How to use Shelfmark

Shelfmark answers two questions that UCLA’s own search leaves to you: which library holds the thing, and which shelf it is on once you are standing in the building.

Where things are

Four pages, and the bar at the top of every one of them goes to all four.

Search
The one box, the catalog and the article index. Start here.
Map
The Biomed shelf map, floor by floor, and the pickup walk planner.
Hours
Opening hours for all 27 UCLA locations.
Databases
UCLA’s whole A to Z list, filterable.

Everything starts in the one box

There used to be a box per question, and you had to know which question you were asking before you could ask it. There is one field now and it decides:

QL737.C22
Anything shaped like a call number is answered on the spot, with the floor and the shelf face and a small map of that floor. One click opens it on the full map.
10.1056/NEJMoa1816897
A DOI, a PubMed ID or an ISSN goes to the article panel and the link resolver.
Everything else
Goes to UCLA’s catalog as a title, author or subject search.

The choice it made is always shown, and always reversible in one click. Guessing wrong quietly would be worse than asking.

Call numbers and the shelf map

Type a call number as printed. Spaces and the Cutter dot are optional, so QL737.C22, QL 737 C22 and W1 JO600 all work. A W1 number needs the space after W1.

The locator finds the shelf whose range contains it, comparing class letters, then class number, then each Cutter as a decimal, so AM4733 sorts before AM477. That last part is the one people trip over: Cutters are decimals, not integers.

Reading the map

  • Each green column is a double-sided shelf, with an L and an R face.
  • Black is the bottom row.
  • A dash means a face nobody has surveyed yet.
  • Where a range start equals its end, many volumes share one call number, so every matching shelf is returned and you check the spine.

Reference is on floor 4 and Special Collections on floor 9. On the map page, switch the Biomed map pill to search those; from the search box, type Hist Div in front of the call number and it goes to Special Collections on its own. Only Biomed stacks copies resolve to a shelf you can walk to; a copy at Powell or YRL is reported as being there, without a shelf face.

A shelf hit in a catalog result is a link. Clicking it opens the map on that floor with the shelf already lit.

Books

Results are scoped to the library you say you are working at, and widen to the rest of UCLA only if yours has nothing. All 21 libraries work.

The catalog ranks nothing and corrects no spelling, so both happen here. A title that matches no record is checked against Primo’s dictionary first and against the catalog’s own vocabulary second, and any correction is named rather than applied quietly. A typo or two still finds the book.

Filters and syntax

The Filters panel is shorthand for tokens you can type instead, and the Search syntax panel in the tool lists all of them. The ones worth knowing by heart:

title: au: su:
Title phrase, author, subject.
mesh:neoplasms
Medical subject heading.
year:2015+
Also year:2010..2020, after:2015, year:1990s.
at:yrl
Search a named library, overriding the pills. at:ucla for everywhere.
cardio*
Trailing truncation. End of a word only.
shelf:yes
Keep only copies that resolve to a real shelf face.

Two words both have to appear. Quote a phrase to require them together.

Articles

Articles come from the same index UCLA’s own search uses, and by default only papers UCLA can actually get you. Narrow by year, kind, language, peer review, whether it is free to read, or to one journal picked off a result.

Every row answers one question: whether you can read the thing.

Free to read
Opens for anybody, with no sign-in and no VPN. The button is green.
Full text via UCLA
The library licenses it. It opens on campus, and off campus after signing in.
In print at UCLA
There is a physical copy. Search its call number to find the shelf.
Not at UCLA
Interlibrary loan can usually get it.

The button takes the best route in and says which door it is. Everything else the library offers for that paper sits behind “other ways in”, because a reader after one paper does not need eight buttons to choose between.

Paste an identifier instead of a title and the link resolver answers who carries the full text, and for which years. Coverage is worth checking before walking someone over: a provider can carry the journal and not the issue.

Databases A–Z

The databases page is UCLA’s whole list — 1,360 of them — taken from the same LibGuides feed the library’s own A to Z page is built from, and refreshed once a day. It is there for the searches this site cannot do: PsycINFO, Web of Science, CINAHL, a newspaper archive, a dataset. Shelfmark searches UCLA’s catalog and its article index; everything else lives behind one of these.

Type in the filter box to narrow the list by name. It matches anywhere in the name, so psyc finds PsycINFO and PsycARTICLES, and pub finds PubMed. Filtering happens in your browser against the list already on screen, so it answers as you type and costs nothing.

Two checkboxes narrow it further:

Best bets only
The library’s own flag on the ones it recommends first for a subject. 340 of the 1,360 carry it.
Open to anyone
Hides everything that asks for a UCLA login, leaving the 106 that open from anywhere. Useful when you are off campus with no VPN, or sending a link to someone who is not at UCLA.

Every entry opens in a new tab, at the same address the library’s page would send you to. A database marked no login should open straight away; everything else will ask you to sign in, on campus or off.

What the feed carries is a name, a link, and those two facts. There are no descriptions in it — the library publishes them on its own page but not through this feed — so this list does not show a description field that would always be empty. If you need to know what a database covers before opening it, the library’s A to Z page has the blurbs.

Hours, and where the buildings are

Opening hours come from LibCal and are read live, so they are as current as the library’s own page. Every UCLA location is listed, not just Biomed. Pick any date within a year back or two years forward; the day strip moves a week at a time.

That page answers when. For where — the question you get asked at a desk, with someone standing in front of you — it also carries UCLA’s campus map of the nine library locations, which is the thing to hold up and point at, and a link to the library’s own Locations & Hours page for the same information in text. The map is UCLA’s, reproduced here so it opens in one tap rather than three.

Pickup walks

At the bottom of the map page, the pickup walk takes a list of call numbers, or photographs of them, and puts them in the order you would walk the stacks rather than the order you typed them. Photographs are read in the browser with optical character recognition; the images are never uploaded anywhere.

Nothing here needs an account. No sign-in, no analytics, no cookies. What you type is sent to UCLA’s public endpoints to answer the search, and nowhere else.
